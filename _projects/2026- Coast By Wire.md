The purpose of this project is to investigate new ways to allow Cornell Electric Vehicles’ (CEV) Urban Concept car to drive backwards, without sacrificing its ability to coast. For reference, CEV is a project team dedicated to building hyper-efficient autonomous electric vehicles. Each year, we build a car to compete in the Shell Eco-marathon, a competition that pushes high school and college students to build the most efficient car possible. To be competitive, most teams use one-way bearings, which allow their vehicles to coast like a bicycle. While these bearings are great for maximizing efficiency, they create a massive issue for our autonomy initiative. In this configuration, the car can only drive forward, which is extremely inconvenient for navigating in small spaces or parking. More specifically, this project will investigate the use of a controllable electromagnetic (EM) clutch as a replacement for a one-way bearing. The final product is a benchtop testing rig design that can be used to validate custom controls. These controls will allow the car to be in one of three modes: forward lock, forward coasting, or reverse lock. In forward lock and reverse lock driving modes, the motor is always connected to the wheels. In coasting mode, the clutch behaves like a one-way bearing.
