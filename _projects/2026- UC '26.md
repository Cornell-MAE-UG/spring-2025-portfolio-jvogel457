Cornell Electric Vehicles (CEV) is a student-run project team dedicated to building hyper-efficient autonomous electric vehicles. Each year we compete in the Shell Eco-marathon at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway with dozens of other universities. This competition pushes teams to design vehicles that are as fuel-efficient as possible. 

In my junior year, I served as the mechanical lead of CEV. As a lead, I oversaw the design, manufacturing, and testing of our drivetrain, steering system, and carbon fiber chassis of our 2026 vehicle. Additionally, I had the privilege of driving the vehicle at competition. 

Big Changes: 
The 2026 vehicle saw massive design changes from previous years. In an attempt to reduce mass, we traded our spare tires for fat-bike tires. While they certainly reduced overall mass, this switch required us to completely redesign our wheel mounting structures. Additionally, we switched from dual-motor drive to one-wheel drive, which reduced mass significantly. Success:
In competition, we completed four valid attempts. In our final attempt, we recorded an efficiency of 37 mi/kWh, which marks an 85% increase from the previous year. That is almost 10 times more efficient than a Tesla!
